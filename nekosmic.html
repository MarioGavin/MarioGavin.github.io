NekosmiCat Dealers

Systems Designer, Economy Programmer & Technical Writer

In-game screenshot: Resource management and ship layout.

NekosmiCat Dealers is a mobile idle resource management game [cite: 23] where players build a criminal empire in space. Working on this project, I took full ownership of the System Design, Economy Balancing, and Monetization, while also programming the core financial logic and authoring the comprehensive Game Design Document (GDD).

Lore & Progression Loop

I wrote the foundational lore to drive player motivation: The protagonist was once the best in the industry until a harsh betrayal by a partner forced them to start from scratch. Accompanied only by a loyal Assistant [cite: 151], the player must progressively unlock and upgrade ship rooms [cite: 26] to reclaim their position at the top.

Economy & Monetization Design

Balancing a management game requires rigorous math and psychological reward loops. I designed and programmed the entire economic infrastructure:

  • Multi-Currency System: Designed a robust economy utilizing four main indicators: Money, Merchandise, Reputation, and Premium Gems[cite: 91].
  • Room Architecture & Upgrades: Conceptualized the function of every room (e.g., Command Room, Bar, Crops, Casino, Hospital)[cite: 113, 114, 116, 117, 122]. I balanced the ROI (Return on Investment) for upgrading these rooms [cite: 68] versus saving resources for defensive measures.
  • Monetization Integration: Designed ethical and engaging mobile monetization hooks, balancing free daily rewards with gem-based refresh systems for hiring workers[cite: 92, 96]. I also programmed the underlying codebase for these financial transactions.
"My goal was to create an economy where every spending decision feels impactful. Players must constantly weigh the risk of investing in illegal merchandise against upgrading defensive systems to protect their assets."

Systemic Worker Mechanics (The "Cats")

To make the management aspect feel alive, I designed a deep simulation system for the cat workers, giving them distinct statistics and psychological states[cite: 163, 171]:

  • Dynamic Stats: Workers have specific health, happiness, and loyalty levels [cite: 171] which directly affect their productivity and salary demands[cite: 167, 169].
  • Emergent Behaviors: If neglected, workers can become sick, depressed, or even turn into Rebels[cite: 172, 174]. The player must actively manage their well-being by building Bedrooms and Bathrooms[cite: 174].
  • The Impostor Threat: I implemented a high-stakes social deduction mechanic where low loyalty can spawn an "Impostor" who will silently eliminate other workers until identified and fired by the player[cite: 176, 178].

Dynamic Risk Events

To disrupt the idle loop and keep players engaged, I designed random raid events[cite: 133]:

  • Police Raids: Players must react quickly to warning signals and manually close illegal operations (like the Casino or Crops) [cite: 116, 117, 137] before the police arrive to avoid heavy fines and confiscations[cite: 135, 139].
  • Pirates & Meteor Rains: External threats require players to invest in passive defenses (Shields and Lasers) to prevent structural damage and economic losses[cite: 140, 142, 145, 146].
